# About This Project

This web app is part of an MSc thesis project developing a parcel-level decision support tool for climate adaptive rainwater management in Kerpen, focused on the Horrem neighbourhood. It combines local flood hazard data, GIS based site analysis, and a set of five nature based solutions, green roof, rain garden, infiltration trench, rainwater cistern, and bioswale, to give private landowners recommendations specific to their own property.

**Goal:**  
To help homeowners in existing residential areas understand which rainwater management measures are actually feasible on their parcel, and which of those options best match their own priorities, whether that's reducing runoff, keeping costs down, or minimising upkeep. The tool is built entirely on publicly available spatial data (soil, terrain, building footprints, land use), so the same approach can be extended to other neighbourhoods and municipalities.


**Methodology:**
The recommendations on this site come from a four-stage GIS-based analysis of Horrem's 494 residential parcels:

1. Screening - Five nature-based solutions (green roof, rain garden, infiltration trench, rainwater cistern, bioswale) were selected from the wider literature based on compatibility with private parcel retrofit and Kerpen's local planning guidance.
2. Suitability mapping - Each parcel was checked against engineering thresholds (slope, soil infiltration capacity, groundwater depth, setback distances) using open spatial datasets, then refined further based on site geometry and available space.
3. Prioritisation -  Feasible options for each parcel were ranked using a multi-criteria analysis (AHP) across three priorities: rainwater retention, cost, and maintenance demand.
4. Communication - Results are presented here in a simplified format so homeowners can see their feasible options without needing GIS or engineering expertise.



**What you'll find here:**
- Which of the five nature based solutions are technically feasible on your parcel, based on soil, slope, groundwater, and site geometry
- A ranking of your feasible options under three different priorities: maximum rainwater retention, lowest cost, and lowest maintenance
- Background on your parcel's flood exposure and the reasoning behind each recommendation


This tool is a research prototype developed as part of a Master's thesis at TH Köln and has not yet been tested with residents. Feedback is welcome as the project develops.
